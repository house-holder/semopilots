122.75

Air-to-Air Frequency


...what about 1-2-3-4-5?

The FAA has an agreement with the FCC to regulate the radio waves we use on a daily basis. The decision was made years ago (ref AC 90-50D) that 122.75 should be an aircraft air-to-air frequency. If you're thinking of using "fingers", don't - it's in the reserved band for flight testing, and you could be interfering.


I tune 122.75 whenever we're working in the practice area. Other instructors and any solo students should be doing the same. You can even use this on solo flights to talk to a friend or ask a friendly CFI what they think about the weather.


Practice Area Ops

To report on air-to-air, you must first know where you are. I made the overlay to help you with this, because I know a lot of our students are not from the local area.


But the goal is not to stare at Foreflight! Instead, learn the local landmarks and report an estimate of your position by looking outside. Hint: Runways are near a "mile" long.


Nobody wants to hear how many tenths of a mile you are from something, so use your powers of reasoning to estimate your distance from a landmark - and stop letting Garmin hold your hand.


If you're heading to a specific area to practice, call it/claim it. If you want to split an area with others, let them know you'll stay "east of X" or "south of Y..." Use your head.



Example Calls:

  • "Academy traffic, Goldwing Twenty-Six Bravo is over Bell City at 3500, steep turns, Academy"
  • "Academy traffic, Goldwing Thirty Bravo south of Cape heading east of Benton for ground ref maneuvers, Academy"
  • "Academy traffic, Goldwing Fifty-Five Bravo aproaching Daisy northbound. Goldwing Nine Alpha Bravo, are you on frequency?"
  • "Academy traffic, Goldwing Twenty Six Bravo leaving Cape for Bird Hill, 3500 and above for airwork.

The goal is to understand each other's positions and intentions. Speak plainly on air-to-air. Just remember: look outside!
